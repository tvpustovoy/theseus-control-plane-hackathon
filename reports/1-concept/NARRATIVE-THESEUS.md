# Theseus Control Plane — opening narrative (draft v1)

*Setu.Hackathon · task D01 · November 7, 2026, 13:21 PST. Owner direction, about 13:19: the point is to help the owner notice
what is shifting and drifting that matters to him, and to prompt his attention — the organization is developing
differently from how he laid it down. This narrative does not depend on the final demo scenario. Russian review page:
`NARRATIVE-THESEUS.html`.*

## Core idea in one line

**Theseus notices where your organization is drifting from the course you set — and brings it to your attention while
it can still be corrected.**

## Stage text — opening (~70 seconds)

> In Athens they kept the ship of Theseus. As its planks rotted, they replaced them, one by one, until no original plank
> was left. And the philosophers asked: is it still the same ship?
>
> That question is now about your company. Agents are replacing the planks — a process here, a role there, code, skills,
> context. Every day, while you sail. That is the point: the ship gets better.
>
> But every new plank is a little different. Each change makes sense on its own. And one day the ship is sailing
> somewhere you never set a course for. Nobody decided it. It drifted.
>
> So the real question is not "is it the same ship". It is "is it still going where I said?"
>
> You cannot inspect every plank. There are thousands, and they change every hour. You need something that watches the
> course with you: that notices what is shifting and matters to you, and says — look here.
>
> That is Theseus Control Plane. It watches your organization in five areas — purpose, rules, resources, relationships
> and progress. It notices where the organization is developing differently from how you laid it down, and brings you
> the one thing worth your attention. You decide. Theseus makes sure the decision takes hold. And because it keeps every
> old plank, you can always compare — and go back.
>
> Let your agents rebuild the ship. Keep the helm.

## Five ways a ship drifts

| Area | How the drift looks | What Theseus helps you notice | What you can do |
|---|---|---|---|
| Purpose | The crew works hard, for a different port | work that no longer serves why the organization exists, or crosses its boundary | restate the purpose or the boundary |
| Rules | The crew has customs nobody approved | living practices that contradict written rules | accept, reject or reshape the practice |
| Resources | Provisions — and your own strength — go where you did not send them | spend, repeated work and owner attention flowing against priorities | move budgets, priorities, autonomy limits |
| Relationships | A promise to a passenger is quietly broken | commitments to customers and people the system no longer keeps | restore the commitment, choose who speaks for you |
| Progress | The crew is learning — the wrong lesson | goals drifting, improvements that optimize the wrong thing | redirect goals; adopt or roll back a change |

Each area has two sides: what the owner sees and preserves, and what the owner directs (the November 4 5×2 framework).
Every decision is traced: received → understood → applied → observed in behavior.

## How drift shows itself: the founder's codex against the flow of deals

Owner direction, shortly after: find the conflict between the founder's principles (the codex) and what actually happens in the
flow of data and deals — exactly what happened in the original project.

**The codex.** The founder said two things about discounts:
1. Discounts can be flexible.
2. We do not flood people with discounts, substituting price for value.

**The flow.** Thousands of deals, stage changes, calls and chats. Each discount looked reasonable to whoever gave it.

**The drift Theseus notices.** Discounts are applied unsystematically: who gets one, how much and why vary without a
pattern the codex would recognize; some are the first answer to a price objection, with no value exchanged. Nobody
decided to abandon principle 2. It drifted, one deal at a time.

**Why it needs a human.** The two principles pull against each other. "Ban discounts" breaks principle 1; "anything
goes" breaks principle 2. Only the founder can say where flexibility ends and value substitution begins.

The same shape appears on Firny's landing (a CRM "closed lost" against a customer email asking for a revised quote): a
contradiction between two records is the first visible crack; Theseus follows it to the pattern, the root cause and
what is at stake, then asks the owner one question. *Firny's and the original project's figures are prior work. On stage
we use only what today's pipeline finds.*

## One drift, five areas

A single drift reads differently in each area — this is how the five areas become visible without five separate demos:

| Area | The discount drift, seen from here |
|---|---|
| Purpose | Are we selling value or price? |
| Rules | The codex says one thing; the practice in the deal flow says another |
| Resources | Margin given away without anything received in return |
| Relationships | Similar customers treated differently; customers learn to wait for a discount |
| Progress | The sales process is "improving" — at discounting faster: the wrong lesson |

## Demo spine (proposal, for discussion with the owner)

1. **Codex.** Meta extracts the founder's two principles from his own words, with citations.
2. **Flow.** Meta extracts discount events and the stated reasons from deals, calls and chats; MongoDB keeps sources,
   claims and versions.
3. **Notice.** SubQ Snap classifies each discount against the codex: consistent · contradicts · different scope ·
   insufficient evidence. Theseus groups the contradictions into one drift and its pattern.
4. **Attention.** The Deputy CEO (Fetch.ai, ASI:One) brings the founder one card: the drift, evidence, the five-area
   reading, what is at stake — and one question.
5. **Decision.** The founder draws the line, e.g. "a discount must buy something of value: a longer term, prepayment,
   a referral; never the first answer to a price objection."
6. **Change.** Gemini builds the versioned rule into the discount-decision tool for the agents and evaluates it on deals
   the builder never saw: value-exchange discounts pass, value-substitution discounts are flagged; the shortcut "no
   discounts at all" is blocked because it breaks principle 1.
7. **Observed.** A new deal arrives; the agent's behavior follows the new version. One step rolls back to the previous
   version.

Every step is shown only if it runs today; the rest is said as "next".
