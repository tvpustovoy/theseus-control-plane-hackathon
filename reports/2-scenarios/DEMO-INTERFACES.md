## The interface we need

One application, with four views: **Commit → Observe → Decide → Track → renew the commitment at the next meeting.**
- **Commit** — confirm the human's intentions, goals and governing principles, and give the control plane an explicit monitoring mandate (step 1).
- **Observe** — observed culture and possible drift against the approved direction (steps 2–3).
- **Decide** — Deputy CEO proposal, three changes and the human decision (step 4).
- **Track** — results of the selected intervention over simulated time (step 5).

A shared evidence drawer opens a source quote, version or run record from any view. Commit and Decide reuse the same version comparison and human-decision components: initial agreement and later correction are two uses of one mechanism. The demo CRM form is part of Decide. Fetch.ai is an additional conversational entrance to the same proposal, not a separate control system.

## Why Commit

**Align** describes the discussion that clarifies intent. **Commit** is the moment a human confirms that intent and the control plane takes on a specific, inspectable responsibility. It also fits the developer audience: an explicit version becomes the reference for later changes.

The commitment has two sides: the human approves the direction and boundaries; the system records what it will monitor, what evidence it can access, when it must surface a concern, and which responses require a human decision. This is a bounded mandate, not a guarantee of goal achievement or complete visibility.

The first screen should make five things clear:
1. **Intent and goals:** what the human wants, why it matters, priorities and success measures.
2. **Governing principles:** the human-approved rules, constraints, exceptions and authority boundaries.
3. **Culture to protect:** which recurring practices support that intent and which emerging norms would create a material risk. Culture remains observed/inferred; approving a principle does not prove that people follow it.
4. **Monitoring commitment:** signals, data coverage, review cadence and criteria for escalation. Missing data stays visible.
5. **Confirmation and state:** an explicit owner decision, committed version and effective time; separately show whether monitoring is configured, partial or active. Saving the mandate alone cannot turn monitoring status to active.

Dangerous cultural drift means an emerging pattern that undermines the approved intent, principles or boundaries, not every cultural change. Examples to assess include discounting becoming the default substitute for explaining value, or agents bypassing approval to hit a target; these are hypotheses until supported by evidence. The control plane surfaces evidence and proposes a correction within its mandate. It does not silently rewrite the human's principles or grant itself enforcement powers.

## The meeting establishes the reference for drift

At a daily or monthly meeting, the owner reviews the intentions, goals and principles the system has identified, corrects their interpretation, and confirms both the direction and the monitoring mandate. The meeting cadence is configurable; the demo needs only one such meeting.

- **Suggested monthly use:** review goals, success measures, resource limits and any proposed codex changes.
- **Suggested daily use:** confirm or change immediate priorities, inspect deviations and approve explicit exceptions. Unchanged principles do not need daily reapproval.
- **On screen:** previous committed version → proposed changes from the meeting → edit / commit / defer. Show the intention, goal, success measure, time horizon, accountable role, priorities, applicable principles and cultural risks to watch. Only the selected changes are approved.
- **Saved reference:** exact approved codex/goal versions, approver, meeting/source reference, scope and effective time. Proposed, approved and applied states remain distinct; a transcript alone is not approval. Agent acknowledgment is separate from demonstrated compliance.

Subsequent observations are compared with the approved version that applied at the time. A newly discussed goal remains a proposal until approved. An approved course change creates a new reference; it is not silently counted as drift or used to rewrite earlier results. Unapproved changes to goals/rules and departures in behavior are drift candidates; simply missing a target does not by itself prove drift. Track brings evidence back to Commit at the next meeting, where the owner can renew or revise the mandate.

### SubQ's concrete responsibility

Commit fixes the human-approved codex and cultural guardrails as versioned rules. Meta extracts observed episodes; deterministic code selects scope, removes exact duplicates and groups repeated behavior. SubQ checks those episodes or patterns against the applicable rules and returns a review signal with evidence references. The visible output belongs in S03, and the same versioned rubric can support S06. The Deputy explains the result and takes the human to the Theseus review screen.

An emerging cultural norm is observed/inferred; it does not become a binding rule just because it repeats. SubQ checks potential violations of the approved codex/guardrails and potentially dangerous cultural drift. It must preserve different-scope and insufficient-evidence outcomes, and does not independently redefine the rules.
